Frequently Asked Questions

How do you monetize a blog?

You monetize a blog by converting traffic into revenue through one or more of these methods: display advertising (ad networks pay $3-$50 per 1,000 pageviews), affiliate marketing (commissions of 1-50% on referred sales), digital products (90-100% margin templates, ebooks, and tools), online courses ($47-$497 per sale), sponsored content ($200-$2,000 per post), consulting ($100-$300 per hour), memberships ($5-$50 per month per member), and newsletter monetization ($1 per subscriber per month). The best approach is a monetization stack combining 3-4 complementary methods.

How much money can you make from a blog?

A blog can earn $100-$500 per month in the first 6 months with display ads and basic affiliate links, $1,000-$5,000 per month after 12 months with multiple revenue streams active, and $5,000-$50,000+ per month for established blogs with 50,000+ monthly visitors using digital products, courses, sponsorships, and affiliate marketing. The average profitable blog earns $1,000-$3,000 per month; the top 10% earn $10,000+. Email lists drive 42% of blog revenue.

What is the best way to monetize a blog?

The best way to monetize a blog is selling your own digital products, because digital products have 90-100% profit margins, no inventory costs, and income is not capped by traffic volume. A blog with 2,000 monthly visitors can earn more selling a $29 template than a 50,000-visitor blog earns from display ads. For beginners under 10,000 monthly visitors, the best starting stack is affiliate marketing plus consulting services.

How much traffic do you need to monetize a blog?

You need zero traffic to monetize through consulting, freelance services, or digital products sold to your existing network. Affiliate marketing becomes viable at 5,000+ monthly visitors. Display ads require 25,000-50,000 monthly pageviews for meaningful income. Sponsored content typically requires 10,000+ monthly visitors. The most profitable blogs monetize through products and services that don't depend on high traffic.

How long does it take to make money from a blog?

It takes 1-30 days if you sell services or consulting to your existing network, 30-90 days if you launch a digital product to a small audience, 3-6 months for affiliate marketing income to compound, 6-12 months for display ad revenue to reach $500+/month, and 12-18 months for a full multi-stream system to generate $3,000+/month consistently. The fastest path to first revenue is selling a digital product to your first 100 email subscribers.

What is blog RPM and how is it calculated?

Blog RPM (revenue per mille) is the amount you earn per 1,000 pageviews. Calculate it as: (Total Revenue / Total Pageviews) x 1,000. Google AdSense RPM ranges from $1-$10, Ezoic $5-$15, Mediavine $15-$40, and Raptive $20-$60. For non-ad revenue, calculate an "effective RPM" by dividing total monthly revenue by monthly pageviews x 1,000. A blog earning $3,000/month from 30,000 pageviews has an effective RPM of $100.

Should I use AdSense or Mediavine for my blog?

Use Google AdSense when starting out (no traffic requirement, $1-$10 RPM) and switch to Mediavine once you reach 50,000 monthly sessions ($15-$40 RPM, 3-5x higher earnings). Ezoic is the best intermediate option — no traffic threshold, $5-$15 RPM, 50-300% better than AdSense. Never stay on AdSense once you qualify for Ezoic or Mediavine; the same traffic earns 3-5x more on premium networks.

5 Prerequisites Before You Monetize

Adding monetization to a blog that isn't ready actively damages your revenue. Ads on a site with 500 monthly visitors earn pennies but slow page speed. Affiliate links in thin content destroy trust. Digital products launched to an empty email list generate zero sales and demoralize you. Check these 5 boxes first:

  1. At least 20 high-quality published posts. Each post should be 1,500+ words, target a specific keyword, and solve a real problem. Thin content repels both readers and advertisers.
  2. Consistent traffic of 1,000+ monthly visitors. Check Google Analytics. If you're under 1,000, focus on content and SEO first. Monetizing before you have readers is premature.
  3. An email list of 100+ subscribers. Your email list is your highest-converting revenue channel. If you don't have one yet, set one up before implementing any monetization — it's that important. Read our guide on how to start a newsletter for the exact setup.
  4. A defined niche and audience. "Personal finance for new parents" monetizes well. "Stuff I find interesting" doesn't. Advertisers, sponsors, and product buyers all need to know who your audience is.
  5. Fast, mobile-optimized site. Page speed directly affects ad RPM and conversion rates. Run your site through PageSpeed Insights and fix any score under 70 before adding monetization layers.
The best blog monetization method for beginners is selling digital products to a small, engaged email list — not display ads. A list of 200 subscribers converting at 3% on a $29 product generates $174 per launch. The same 200 people viewing an ad page generate $0.60. Products beat ads by 290x on small audiences.

1. Display Advertising

Passive income Low effort

Display ads are the easiest monetization method to set up and the lowest-paying per visitor. You insert ad code from an ad network, advertisers fill the slots automatically, and you earn revenue based on pageviews. The math is simple but sobering: RPM (revenue per 1,000 pageviews) determines everything.

Realistic math: A blog with 50,000 monthly pageviews on Mediavine at $25 RPM earns $1,250/month. The same blog on AdSense at $4 RPM earns $200. That 6x difference is why graduating from AdSense to a premium network is the single biggest ad revenue leap you can make.

When to use ads: Display ads work best for high-traffic blogs (25,000+ pageviews) with general-interest content where readers don't have strong purchase intent — lifestyle, news, entertainment, recipes. If your content naturally drives buying decisions (tech reviews, finance recommendations), affiliate marketing and digital products will outperform ads by 5-10x.

Common mistake: Don't over-place ads. More ad slots don't equal more revenue — they reduce page speed, degrade user experience, and can lower RPM. Premium networks like Mediavine optimize placement automatically. If using AdSense or Ezoic, limit to 3-4 ad placements per page.

2. Affiliate Marketing

High upside Medium effort

Affiliate marketing is recommending products and earning a commission when readers buy through your link. It's the highest-upside monetization method for blogs with commercial-intent content (reviews, comparisons, tutorials, "best of" lists). The key advantage over ads: affiliate income scales with purchase value, not just pageviews.

3. Digital Products

Highest margin Medium effort

Digital products — templates, spreadsheets, ebooks, swipe files, printables, Notion templates — are the highest-margin monetization method in existence. Create once, sell infinitely, no inventory, no shipping, no per-unit cost. Profit margins are 90-100% after platform fees. Platforms like Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy, and Payhip handle payment and delivery automatically.

4. Online Courses

Highest ceiling High effort

Online courses are the highest-ceiling monetization method. A single course priced at $197 selling to 100 students generates $19,700 per launch. With an email list of 3,000 subscribers and a 2% conversion rate, each launch produces 60 sales = $11,820. Two launches per year = $23,640 from one product.

Platform comparison:

  • Gumroad — Simplest, lowest overhead, 10% fee. Best for smaller creators selling downloadable courses.
  • Teachable / Thinkific — Full-featured, branded experience, hosted video. $39-$99/month.
  • Podia — All-in-one (courses + memberships + downloads). $33-$75/month.
  • Kajabi — Premium, built-in email marketing and funnel builder. $149+/month.

Realistic math: First-year course creators typically earn $5,000-$15,000 from their first course. Established bloggers with 10,000+ email subscribers earn $20,000-$50,000 per launch. The key is building an audience first — courses sell to people who already trust you, not to cold traffic.

Creation timeline: 4-12 weeks for a complete video course. Start by teaching your skill 1-on-1 (free or cheap) 5 times. Notice the common questions and struggles. Structure those into a course outline. Record video lessons using Loom or your phone. Price your first course at $47-$197.

5. Sponsored Content

High per-post rate Medium effort

Sponsored content is when brands pay you to feature their product in a blog post, newsletter, or social media. B2B niches (software, marketing, finance) pay 5-10x more than B2C lifestyle niches. A blog with 20,000 monthly visitors in a B2B niche can charge $500-$2,000 per sponsored post.

Sponsorship rate formula:

  • Sponsored blog post: $200-$2,000 depending on traffic and niche
  • Newsletter sponsorship: $50 per 1,000 subscribers (B2C), $100-$200 per 1,000 (B2B)
  • Dedicated email: $100-$500 per send
  • Social media mention: $50-$500 per post

Realistic math: 2 sponsored posts/month at $750 each + 2 newsletter sponsors/month at $300 each = $2,100/month from a blog with 15,000 monthly visitors and a 3,000-subscriber email list.

Finding sponsors: Reach out directly to tools and products you already use and mention organically. List your blog on sponsorship marketplaces (Paved, Sponsy, Creator.co). Create a "Work With Me" page with your media kit including audience demographics, traffic stats, and pricing. Use professional email templates for your sponsorship outreach.

Fastest to revenue Low effort

Consulting and services are the fastest path to meaningful blog income at low traffic levels. If your blog demonstrates expertise, readers will pay for your time. This method works with as few as 500 monthly visitors — you only need 1-2 clients per month to generate significant revenue.

7. Membership & Community

Recurring revenue High effort

Memberships create predictable, recurring revenue — the most valuable type of blog income. Members pay monthly for premium content, private community access, office hours, or early access to your work. The model requires an engaged audience (typically 5,000+ monthly visitors) but produces the most stable income once established.

Platforms: Patreon ($5-$30/month tiers), Circle (premium community), Memberful (membership site), Substack (paid newsletter tiers), Beehiiv (paid newsletter).

Pricing: $5-$30/month for most content memberships. $50-$200/month for professional communities with direct access to you. The value proposition must be clear: what does the member get that free readers don't?

Realistic math: 200 members at $15/month = $3,000/month in predictable recurring revenue. 100 members at $49/month = $4,900/month. Membership revenue compounds because monthly churn in well-run communities is under 5%.

8. Newsletter Monetization

Owned audience Medium effort

Your email list is the single most valuable blog asset because you own it — no algorithm can throttle your reach. Email drives 42% of total blog revenue for profitable blogs, making newsletter growth the highest-leverage monetization activity. The benchmark: each email subscriber generates approximately $1 per month across all monetization methods combined.

Newsletter revenue models:

  • Sponsorships: $50-$200 per 1,000 subscribers per email. A 5,000-subscriber newsletter with 2 sponsorships/month at $250 each = $500/month.
  • Affiliate recommendations: Product recommendations to warm subscribers convert 3-5x better than blog traffic. A single "tool I use" email can generate $200-$1,000 in affiliate commissions.
  • Product launches: Your email list converts at 2-5% on product launches vs 0.5-1% for cold traffic. 2,000 subscribers x 3% conversion x $29 product = $1,740 per launch.
  • Paid subscriptions: Charge $5-$15/month for premium issues. 5% of free subscribers typically convert to paid. 2,000 free subscribers x 5% x $10/month = $1,000/month recurring.

9. SaaS & Micro-SaaS

Recurring + scalable Very high effort

If you have development skills, building a micro-SaaS product is the highest long-term monetization method for a blog. A focused software tool that solves one specific problem for your niche audience generates recurring monthly revenue with 80-95% margins. Examples: an SEO audit tool for marketing blogs, a portfolio tracker for finance blogs, a workout tracker for fitness blogs.

Realistic math: 100 users at $19/month = $1,900/month. 500 users at $29/month = $14,500/month. Revenue compounds because churn is low (5-8% monthly) in well-built niche tools. Launch using conversion-optimized landing page templates to go live in a day.

When to build SaaS: Only after you've validated demand through consulting or digital products. If readers repeatedly ask for a tool that doesn't exist, that's your micro-SaaS. Build the simplest possible version (MVP) in 4-8 weeks, charge from day one, and get 10 paying users before adding features.

10. Content Licensing & Syndication

Passive Low effort

Content licensing is selling the rights to republish your blog content on other platforms. Media companies, aggregators, and industry publications pay for high-quality content in specific niches. This is a passive income stream that requires no additional work after the initial content is published.

Revenue range: $50-$500 per licensed article. Syndication deals with platforms like Medium Partner Program or industry-specific aggregators can generate $200-$3,000/month for blogs with a large content library. The key is having a catalog of 50+ high-quality posts that other platforms want to republish.

11. Blog Flipping

Large one-time payout Medium effort

Blog flipping is building a blog to a profitable level and selling it. Profitable blogs sell for 30-40x monthly revenue on marketplaces like Flippa and Empire Flippers. A blog generating $2,000/month sells for $60,000-$80,000. A blog generating $5,000/month sells for $150,000-$200,000.

What makes a blog sellable: Consistent monthly revenue (6+ months of data), diversified traffic sources (not 100% Google-dependent), clean financials, transferable systems (email list, content calendar, SOPs). Blogs with a single revenue stream sell for lower multiples; multi-stream blogs sell for premium multiples.

Realistic math: Build a blog to $2,000/month over 18 months, sell for $70,000. That's equivalent to earning $3,888/month for 18 months — but you also earned the $2,000/month during those 18 months. Total: $106,000 over 18 months from one blog.

12. Paid Directory or Job Board

Niche revenue Medium effort

If your blog serves a specific industry or community, a paid directory or job board can generate significant revenue. Companies pay $50-$500 per listing to reach your audience. This works best for B2B blogs, local blogs, and niche professional communities.

Examples: A marketing blog could host a directory of marketing tools. A freelance blog could host a job board for freelance gigs. A local blog could host a business directory for the community.

Realistic math: 20 paid listings/month at $100 each = $2,000/month. Job boards typically charge $50-$300 per job posting. Directories charge $50-$500 per listing. Use WordPress plugins like WP Job Manager or Simple Directory to set up quickly.

3 Proven Monetization Stacks

The biggest monetization mistake is trying to activate all 12 methods at once. Successful blogs build a stack — a combination of 3-4 complementary methods that match their traffic level and content type. Here are three proven stacks for different stages:

Stack 1: Beginner (0-5,000 monthly visitors)

Affiliate marketing + Consulting + Email list building

At this stage, you don't have enough traffic for ads or sponsorships to matter. Focus on the two methods that don't require traffic: affiliate marketing (write review/comparison content that ranks for buyer-intent keywords) and consulting (add a "Hire Me" page and offer services). Build your email list aggressively — every visitor should have a capture opportunity. Target: $500-$1,500/month.

Stack 2: Growth (5,000-25,000 monthly visitors)

Affiliate marketing + Digital products + Newsletter sponsorships + Ezoic ads

At this stage, you have enough traffic for affiliate income to compound, a large enough audience to sell digital products, and enough email subscribers to attract newsletter sponsors. Add Ezoic ads (no traffic threshold, $5-$15 RPM) for passive baseline revenue. Launch your first digital product. Start pitching newsletter sponsors at 1,000+ subscribers. Target: $2,000-$5,000/month.

Stack 3: Full-Time (25,000+ monthly visitors)

Mediavine ads + Digital products + Online course + Sponsored posts + Newsletter sponsorships

At this stage, you qualify for premium ad networks ($15-$40 RPM), have enough audience to fill a course launch, and attract inbound sponsorship inquiries. Your monetization stack should include 4-5 active revenue streams. The email list is the connective tissue — it drives product launches, sponsorship value, and course sales. Target: $5,000-$20,000+/month.

The best blog monetization stack for most bloggers is: digital products + affiliate marketing + email newsletter sponsorships. This combination generates revenue at any traffic level, has 90%+ margins, doesn't depend on a single platform, and compounds over time as your email list and content library grow.

Your 90-Day Monetization Action Plan

Here's a concrete 90-day plan to go from zero monetization to your first revenue streams. This assumes you already have 20+ blog posts and 1,000+ monthly visitors. If you don't, focus on content and SEO first.

Phase 1: Foundation (Days 1-30)

  • Day 1-5: Audit your existing content. Identify your 5 highest-traffic posts and 5 posts with the most commercial intent (reviews, tutorials, "how to" posts). These are your monetization anchors.
  • Day 6-10: Set up your email list if you haven't. Use Beehiiv (free up to 2,500 subscribers) or ConvertKit. Add a lead magnet (free checklist, template, or mini-course) and place opt-in forms in your top 5 posts and sidebar.
  • Day 11-15: Join 3-5 affiliate programs relevant to your niche. Start with Amazon Associates (easy approval), then add 2-3 SaaS or course affiliate programs with recurring commissions. Add affiliate links naturally to your 5 anchor posts.
  • Day 16-20: Create a "Hire Me" or "Services" page. Define 1-2 consulting or service offerings. Add a Calendly booking link. Link to it from your sidebar and About page.
  • Day 21-30: If you have 25,000+ pageviews, apply to Mediavine. If under 25,000, switch from AdSense to Ezoic. Write your first affiliate review post targeting a buyer-intent keyword.

Phase 2: First Revenue (Days 31-60)

  • Day 31-35: Identify a problem your readers repeatedly ask about. Outline a digital product that solves it (template, ebook, or swipe file). Price it at $9-$29.
  • Day 36-45: Build the digital product. Use tools you already know (Excel, Google Docs, Canva, Notion). Don't over-engineer it — a 20-page PDF or a 5-tab spreadsheet solves a real problem and generates real revenue.
  • Day 46-50: Set up a Gumroad account and list your product. Write a sales page using the copywriting formulas from our swipe file guide.
  • Day 51-55: Write a blog post that naturally leads to your product. Add a CTA box at the end. Email your list about the launch (even if it's only 100 subscribers).
  • Day 56-60: Pitch 5 potential sponsors for your newsletter. Use the cold outreach email templates from our Email Template Playbook. Target: 1-2 sponsor agreements.

Phase 3: Scale (Days 61-90)

  • Day 61-70: Write 3 more affiliate review posts targeting buyer-intent keywords. Each post should target a keyword with 500+ monthly searches and link to 2-3 affiliate products. Optimize for SEO using proper title tags, meta descriptions, and internal links.
  • Day 71-80: Create a content upgrade for your top 3 posts (a free downloadable resource specific to each post). This grows your email list 3-5x faster than a generic lead magnet.
  • Day 81-85: Analyze your revenue by stream. Which method generated the most revenue? Double down on that. Which generated the least? Pause or reduce effort there.
  • Day 86-90: Plan your next 90 days. If digital products are working, plan product #2. If affiliate marketing is winning, write 5 more review posts. If consulting is your top earner, systemize your service delivery. The goal is to compound what's working.
Remember: The blogs that make real money don't have more traffic than you — they have better monetization stacks. A blog with 10,000 monthly visitors earning $3,000/month from digital products and affiliate marketing out-earns a blog with 100,000 visitors making $400/month from AdSense. Focus on the stack, not the traffic. Build your email list first, layer in 2-3 revenue streams that match your audience, and compound from there.
