The 7 KPIs Every Small Business Should Track

There are hundreds of KPIs you could track. Most are noise. After analysing what small businesses actually need to make decisions, seven KPIs consistently matter across industries — from SaaS to e-commerce to consulting to local services. Here they are, with how to calculate each one.

1. Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR)

MRR is the predictable revenue you expect every month. For subscription businesses, it's the sum of all active subscriptions. For non-subscription businesses, calculate it as the average monthly revenue over the trailing 12 months. MRR is the single most important number for understanding the baseline health of a business because it strips out one-off windfalls and shows what's sustainable.

Formula: MRR = Sum of active monthly subscription values (or trailing 12-month average for non-subscription).

2. Revenue Growth Rate

MRR tells you where you are. Growth rate tells you where you're going. Calculate it month-over-month to spot trends early. A 5% monthly growth rate compounds to 80% annually — that's healthy. A 0% growth rate means your business is flat, and flat businesses don't survive inflation.

Formula: Growth Rate = (This Month MRR − Last Month MRR) / Last Month MRR × 100

3. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

CAC is how much you spend to acquire one new customer. If you spend $1,000 on ads and get 10 customers, your CAC is $100. If each customer is worth $300, you're profitable. If each is worth $50, you're losing money on every acquisition — a common trap that kills businesses that scale marketing before checking the math.

Formula: CAC = Total Marketing & Sales Spend / New Customers Acquired

4. Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)

LTV is the total revenue a customer generates before they leave. It's the number that tells you whether your CAC is sustainable. The universal benchmark is a 3:1 LTV-to-CAC ratio — a customer should be worth at least 3x what it cost to acquire them. If your LTV is $600 and CAC is $200, you're healthy. If LTV is $150 and CAC is $200, every new customer is destroying value.

Formula: LTV = Average Revenue per Customer per Month × Average Customer Lifespan (months)

5. Monthly Churn Rate

Churn is the percentage of customers who leave each month. For subscription businesses, this is the number that keeps founders awake. A 5% monthly churn rate means you lose 46% of your customers every year — you're pouring water into a leaky bucket. A 2% monthly churn rate means you lose 22% annually, which is manageable. Track churn alongside growth: if you're growing 5% but churning 5%, you're treading water, not growing.

Formula: Churn Rate = Customers Lost This Month / Total Customers at Start of Month × 100

6. Sales Pipeline Value

Pipeline value is the total value of deals currently in your sales pipeline, weighted by their probability of closing. A $50,000 pipeline at 30% average probability is worth $15,000 in expected revenue. This tells you whether you have enough opportunities to hit next month's target — or whether you need to start prospecting urgently.

Formula: Weighted Pipeline = Σ (Deal Value × Probability for each open deal)

7. Marketing ROI by Channel

Marketing ROI measures how much revenue each marketing channel generates for every dollar spent. If LinkedIn ads produce $5 for every $1 spent (5x ROI) and Google Ads produce $1.50 for every $1 spent (1.5x ROI), you should reallocate budget immediately. Without per-channel ROI tracking, you're guessing — and guessing with ad spend is how businesses burn cash.

Formula: Channel ROI = Revenue Attributed to Channel / Channel Spend

If you track only one number, track MRR. If you track seven, track these seven. They cover the full business cycle: revenue in, growth rate, acquisition cost, customer value, retention, pipeline, and marketing efficiency.

How to Build a KPI Dashboard in Excel (Step by Step)

If you want to understand how a KPI dashboard works under the hood, here's how to build one from scratch. (If you'd rather skip the setup, download the ready-made template and jump to the comparison section.)

Step 1: Create a Revenue Tracker Tab

Set up a 12-month revenue table with columns for Month, MRR, New Revenue, Churned Revenue, Net Growth, Cumulative Revenue, and Growth Rate %. Use formulas to calculate growth rate month-over-month and add a line chart showing MRR trend and a bar chart for new vs. churned revenue. This tab feeds the revenue KPIs on your dashboard.

Step 2: Create a Customer Metrics Tab

Track 12 months of customer data: New Customers, Churned Customers, Total Customers, CAC (marketing spend / new customers), LTV (based on average revenue per customer and average lifespan), and Churn Rate. Add charts showing customer growth and churn rate trend. This tab feeds the customer KPIs — the numbers that tell you whether your business model is sustainable.

Step 3: Create a Sales Pipeline Tab

Set up a deal tracker with columns for Deal Name, Contact, Value, Stage (dropdown: Lead, Contacted, Replied, Confirmed, Closed Won, Closed Lost), Probability %, Weighted Value (auto-calculated), and Close Date. Add a summary that sums weighted values by stage and a chart showing pipeline value by stage. This tab feeds the pipeline KPI that tells you whether you have enough in the funnel to hit your targets.

Step 4: Create a Marketing Metrics Tab

Track 10 marketing channels with columns for Channel, Monthly Spend, Leads Generated, New Customers, CAC, Revenue Attributed, and ROI. Use a pie chart to show spend distribution and a bar chart to show ROI by channel. This tab feeds the marketing KPI that tells you where to invest and where to cut.

Step 5: Create a Goals & Targets Tab

Set up 6 annual KPI targets (e.g., $500K revenue, 200 new customers, <3% churn, $150 CAC, 4x LTV:CAC, $2M pipeline). For each, enter the target value, current value, and a formula that calculates progress percentage. Apply conditional formatting: green if on track (>80% of annual target pace), amber if behind (50–80%), red if at risk (<50%). This tab gives you the traffic-light status that makes the dashboard scannable in seconds.

Step 6: Build the Dashboard Tab

This is where everything comes together. Pull the headline number from each metric tab using cross-tab formulas: =SUMIFS(Revenue[Net Growth], Revenue[Month], "<current month>") for revenue, ='Customer Metrics'!C20 for CAC, and so on. Display each as a KPI card — a bordered cell with the metric name, the value, and a comparison to last month or to target. Add the revenue trend chart, a pipeline funnel summary, and the goal status indicators. Apply conditional formatting so any KPI that's behind target turns red.

That's a working KPI dashboard in six tabs. The problem is that building it from scratch takes 6–10 hours of Excel work, and the cross-tab formulas, chart placement, and conditional formatting rules are easy to get wrong. That's why a pre-built template is worth it — you skip the setup and start entering data immediately.

Common KPI Dashboard Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)

1. Tracking Too Many KPIs

The most common mistake is tracking 20+ KPIs because "more data is better." It isn't. A dashboard with 20 metrics is a wall of numbers that nobody reads. Fix: Track the seven KPIs above. If you can't act on a metric weekly, it doesn't belong on the dashboard. Keep it on a background tab if you need it for quarterly reviews.

2. Tracking Vanity Metrics

Page views, social media followers, and email list size are vanity metrics — they feel good but don't drive decisions. Fix: Replace them with actionable metrics. Instead of "email list size," track "email conversion rate." Instead of "page views," track "revenue per visitor." If a metric doesn't change a decision you'd make, it's not a KPI.

3. Not Updating the Dashboard Regularly

A KPI dashboard only works if the data is current. If you update it quarterly, you're doing quarterly reviews, not KPI management. Fix: Set a weekly 15-minute calendar block to enter the week's data. A good template makes this fast — you're entering numbers into pre-formatted tabs, not building formulas.

4. Ignoring Trends for Snapshots

A single month's number tells you nothing. MRR of $30,000 in isolation is meaningless — is it up from $28,000 or down from $35,000? Fix: Always look at trends, not snapshots. A good template includes 12-month charts for every KPI so you can see direction, not just position.

5. Not Connecting KPIs to Actions

A dashboard that shows churn at 8% but doesn't trigger a retention campaign is just a report. Fix: For each KPI, define what action you'll take if it's off-target. If CAC exceeds $150, pause the unprofitable ad channel. If churn exceeds 5%, launch a win-back campaign. The dashboard should drive decisions, not just display data.

When to Switch from Excel to a Paid BI Tool

An Excel KPI dashboard isn't forever. As your business grows, there comes a point where a spreadsheet's limitations become real friction. Here are the signals that it's time to consider migrating to a paid BI tool:

  • You need real-time data from live databases, APIs, or cloud platforms. Excel requires manual data entry; Power BI and Tableau connect automatically.
  • You have a team that needs simultaneous access. Excel's file-sharing model breaks down when 5 people need to view the dashboard at once.
  • You're connecting more than 5 data sources. If you're pulling from Stripe, Google Analytics, Facebook Ads, Salesforce, and a database, a BI tool's connectors save hours of manual export/import work.
  • You need automated refresh. If your data changes hourly and the dashboard needs to reflect it, manual Excel updates won't keep up.
  • You need advanced visualisations like geo-mapping, scatter plots with trend lines, or interactive drill-downs that Excel's chart engine can't do well.

If you hit two or more of these, start evaluating paid BI tools. But even then, your Excel dashboard isn't wasted — it's the prototype that defines what you need. You'll know exactly which KPIs matter, what charts you want, and what the dashboard should look like, which makes configuring a BI tool far faster.
