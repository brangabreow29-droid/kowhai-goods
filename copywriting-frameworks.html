15 Copywriting Frameworks That Convert (With Real Examples)

Published September 2026 · 13 min read

The best copywriting framework is PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution) — it works in every format, from cold email to landing pages, because it forces you to lead with the reader's problem instead of your product. But no single framework fits every job: AIDA outperforms PAS on landing pages, FAB wins for product descriptions, and PASTOR is the one for high-ticket sales pages. This guide covers all 15, with real worked examples and a chooser table, so you always know which framework to reach for.

A copywriting framework is not a creative straitjacket — it's a decision sequence. Buyers move through predictable psychological steps (notice, care, believe, act) before they spend money, and each framework encodes that sequence in a different order of emphasis. Copywriters who use frameworks write 3-5x faster and convert measurably better, because the structure does the strategic thinking before a single sentence gets written. Here's every framework that matters, when to use it, and what it looks like fully written out.

Copywriting Framework FAQs

What is a copywriting framework?

A repeatable structural formula — like AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) or PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solution) — that tells you what to say first, second, and third so your copy moves a reader from attention to action. Frameworks encode the psychological sequence people need before buying, instead of leaving you staring at a blank page hoping inspiration shows up.

What are the best copywriting frameworks?

AIDA for landing pages and long-form sales copy, PAS for cold email and ads, FAB for product descriptions, BAB for transformation stories, and PASTOR for high-ticket sales pages. The most useful all-rounder is PAS, because it works across every format and forces you to lead with the reader's problem.

What does AIDA stand for in copywriting?

Attention, Interest, Desire, Action. Hook attention with a headline, build interest with relevant information, intensify desire with outcomes and proof, and close with one clear action. Written down by advertising pioneer E. St. Elmo Lewis in 1898, AIDA is the oldest and still the most widely used copywriting framework for landing pages, sales letters, and email.

What is the PAS framework in copywriting?

PAS means Problem, Agitate, Solution: name a problem the reader already feels, twist the knife by showing its real cost, then present your product as the solution. PAS outperforms other frameworks in cold email and paid ads because it front-loads the reader's pain — the only thing a stranger will read past line one for.

How do I choose the right copywriting framework?

Match two things: the format you're writing (cold email, landing page, social post, sales page) and how aware the reader already is of the problem and your solution. Unaware or problem-aware readers need PAS; solution-aware readers respond to FAB or BAB; product-aware readers need offers and proof, so use PASTOR or a Serial Structure. When unsure, default to PAS.

What is the difference between AIDA and PAS?

AIDA builds interest before desire — stronger for readers who already chose to be there (landing pages, sales letters, warm email). PAS leads with pain — stronger for strangers you're interrupting (cold email, ads, social). Practical rule: if the reader opted in, use AIDA; if you're interrupting them, use PAS.

Can I use copywriting frameworks with AI tools like ChatGPT?

Yes — frameworks make AI output dramatically better. Paste the structure into your prompt ("write a PAS cold email where the problem is X, the agitation is Y, the solution is Z") and the AI produces a coherent first draft instead of generic mush. The framework does the strategic thinking; the AI does the typing.

What Is a Copywriting Framework, Really?

Strip away the acronyms and every copywriting framework is an answer to one question: what does this reader need to hear next, before they'll act? The framework is just the answer written down in advance.

That's why frameworks beat blank-page writing. When you write without a structure, you instinctively start with what you want to say ("We're a leading provider of…"). When you write with a framework, you start with what the reader is already thinking — a problem, a desire, a doubt — and sequence everything after it. The difference in conversion isn't subtle: ads written on a PAS structure routinely beat feature-first equivalents on the same budget purely because of the order of information.

There are three families of copywriting frameworks, and knowing the families makes all 15 easy to remember:

  • Problem-first frameworks (PAS, APP) — open on the reader's pain. Best for strangers: cold email, paid ads, social hooks.
  • Sequence frameworks (AIDA, BAB, FAB, 4 Ps, QUEST, ACCA) — walk the reader from attention to action in order. Best for formats the reader chose to engage with: landing pages, sales letters, product pages.
  • Story frameworks (StoryBrand, Hook-Story-Offer, Serial Structure, STAR, PASTOR) — use narrative to carry belief. Best for high-consideration sales pages, testimonials, case studies, and email sequences.

The Core Four (With Fully Written Examples)

Four copywriting frameworks do 90% of the work in professional copy: AIDA, PAS, FAB, and BAB. Master these before touching the others.

1. AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

Best copywriting framework for landing pages. AIDA is a straight line from the first glance to the buy button. The 1898 granddaddy of all copywriting frameworks still holds up because attention, interest, desire, and action is genuinely the sequence a human being needs.

AIDA in practice — time-tracking app landing page

Attention: Your team bills 31 hours a week. Your timesheets say 19.

Interest: It's not laziness — it's the 90 seconds of context-switching between every task. TimelyTag watches your calendar, commits, and tabs, and reconstructs the real day automatically. No timers, no start/stop buttons.

Desire: Teams switch and recover an average of 7.4 billable hours per person in the first month. That's roughly $1,100 of recovered revenue per employee — before you've configured anything.

Action: Connect your calendar free. First 30 days on us; you'll see the gap in your first week.

Notice the shape: the headline is a claim the reader instantly recognises as their own problem (attention), the second paragraph explains why the problem exists (interest), the third converts the solution into money (desire), and the last is a single, low-friction next step (action). One action, never two.

2. PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution

Best copywriting framework for cold email. PAS is the workhorse of direct response: name the pain, make the pain cost something real, then resolve it. The agitation step is where most people go soft — it's also where the conversion happens.

PAS in practice — B2B cold email

Problem: Most SaaS companies I audit are losing 8-12% of signups between the pricing page and the checkout button.

Agitate: At 400 signups a month, that's 40 paying customers quietly walking away — about $14K in MRR per month, or $170K a year, on a leak nobody owns. Marketing blames the product; the product blames marketing.

Solution: We fixed this exact leak for Hostmetrics in 11 days: same traffic, 9% more completed checkouts. I mapped your signup flow against three patterns we know cause this — one of them is on your pricing page right now. Want me to send the 30-second screen recording?

Every element is about the reader: their leak, their revenue, their pricing page. The sender's credentials appear only inside the proof ("we fixed this for Hostmetrics"), never as the opening move. That's PAS discipline.

3. FAB — Feature, Advantage, Benefit

Best copywriting framework for product descriptions. FAB is the bridge that stops you writing specs and starts you writing what the specs do for the buyer. The advantage is what the feature does that competitors don't; the benefit is why the reader's life gets better.

FAB in practice — standing desk

Feature: Dual motors lift the desktop 4 cm per second at full height.

Advantage: It adjusts faster and quieter than any single-motor desk — so you actually switch positions during calls instead of waiting it out.

Benefit: Standing 3x more often means fewer back-pain afternoons — and the energy dip that used to cost you your 2pm focus.

Spec sheets list the feature. Good product pages add the advantage. Great ones land the benefit. Run every product bullet through FAB and you'll never write "ergonomic design" again.

4. BAB — Before, After, Bridge

Best copywriting framework for transformation stories and email openers. BAB skips logic and goes straight for contrast: paint the current reality, paint the outcome, and put your product in the gap.

BAB in practice — course email

Before: Eighteen months ago, Priya was spending her Sundays hand-invoicing in Word and getting paid, on average, 23 days late.

After: Today her invoices go out automatically on delivery, and her average payment time is 6 days. She hasn't opened Word since March.

Bridge: The difference wasn't discipline — it was a 20-minute setup in our invoice template that she learned on a Tuesday evening. That setup is lesson 2 of the Freelance Business Blueprint, and you can watch it free today.

BAB works because the human brain is a comparison engine — the reader automatically places themselves on the "before" side and wants the "after". The bridge just has to be believable.

The Persuasion Heavyweights: 4 Ps, PASTOR, and QUEST

When the price is high or the decision is slow, the core four aren't enough. These three copywriting frameworks add the belief-building machinery a $1,000+ purchase requires.

5. 4 Ps — Promise, Picture, Proof, Push

The long-form sales letter framework. Open with a promise the reader wants, expand it into a vivid picture of life with the promise kept, stack proof until doubt is untenable, then push with a deadline or scarcity that's real. The 4 Ps is AIDA with desire and action upgraded for big promises.

6. PASTOR — Problem, Amplify, Story, Transformation, Offer, Response

The best copywriting framework for high-ticket sales pages. Ray Edwards' framework adds the two things expensive purchases need that PAS lacks: a story that makes the solution feel human, and a transformation statement that lets the buyer rehearse the future. A coaching programme selling for $3,000+ almost always needs a PASTOR-structured page — problem the prospect admits, amplified cost, a founder story, the transformation with a named before/after, the offer with payment options, then a direct response ask.

7. QUEST — Qualify, Understand, Educate, Stimulate, Transition

The B2B consultant's copywriting framework. QUEST opens by qualifying the reader ("if you run an agency with 5-50 staff…"), shows you understand their specific situation, educates with genuinely useful insight, stimulates desire with the application of that insight, then transitions to the next step. It converts because educated buyers trust sellers who taught them something before asking for anything.

The Modern Attention Frameworks

Attention got scarcer, and newer copywriting frameworks optimise for the first three seconds: feeds, inboxes with 47 unread, and autoplay.

  • 8. Hook-Story-Offer (HSO): the direct-response ad framework — stop the scroll with a pattern break, hold it with a mini-story, then make one offer. Works on YouTube pre-roll, TikTok, and VSLs, because it assumes zero goodwill and builds it inside 90 seconds.
  • 9. APP — Align, Problem, Personal: built for subject lines and social hooks. Align with what the reader already believes, name the shared problem, then make it personal ("the metric your boss will ask about"). APP is why "we both know cold email doesn't work like it used to" outperforms "Introducing our new…"
  • 10. Slack Method: the anti-copywriting framework — write like a message to a coworker: relatable, personal, short. Native-feeding social copy gets read because it doesn't scan as marketing until the third line, by which time you're hooked.
  • 11. Serial Structure: the email-sequence framework. Open a loop, pay part of it off, open a new loop, and push the payoff to the next email. "Yesterday I told you the first mistake. The second one cost me $11,000 and I only noticed it in the audit…" — that's a serial structure working on you right now.
  • 12. ACCA — Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action: the complex-product framework. When the buyer doesn't know the category exists (first CRMs, early no-code tools), you must first make them aware the problem is a problem, then help them comprehend it, then build conviction with proof, then act. ACCA is AIDA stretched for genuinely new markets.

The Story Frameworks: STAR and StoryBrand

13. STAR — Situation, Task, Action, Result

Borrowed from interview technique, STAR is the best copywriting framework for case studies and testimonials because it makes proof legible: where the client was, what needed doing, what you did, and the number at the end. A case study that doesn't follow STAR buries the result; one that does lets a skimmer find it.

14. StoryBrand 7-Part Framework

Donald Miller's structure reframes your whole website as a story where the customer is the hero and your brand is the guide: a character who wants something, has a problem, meets a guide, who gives them a plan, calls them to action, that helps them avoid failure, and ends in success. Most business websites fail because the brand casts itself as the hero; StoryBrand fixes the casting. It's less a page framework than a messaging audit you run before choosing a page framework like AIDA or PASTOR.

Key takeaway: Frameworks stack. A StoryBrand message can be delivered through an AIDA landing page, opened by a PAS cold email, and closed in a Serial Structure sequence. Pick one per piece of copy — never two per piece.

7 Mistakes That Ruin Good Copywriting Frameworks

  1. Leading with the product instead of the reader. If line one is about you, PAS has already failed. The reader's problem is the only opener that earns line two.
  2. Skipping the agitate step. Problem→Solution with no agitation is an FYI, not persuasion. The agitation is where the reader realises the cost of doing nothing.
  3. Stacking frameworks in one piece. AIDA-then-PAS-then-BAB inside one email reads like three writers fighting. One framework per piece of copy, always.
  4. Making up the benefit. FAB's benefit has to be something the buyer would actually say out loud. "Streamline your workflows" is not a sentence any human has spoken.
  5. Using a story framework with no stakes. StoryBrand and Hook-Story-Offer die without a failure to avoid. If the story has no cost, there's no tension, and the copy is a testimonial wearing a costume.
  6. Ignoring awareness stage. AIDA to an unaware audience is a speech in an empty room; Hook-Story-Offer to a most-aware audience wastes attention on belief they already have.
  7. Never testing the framework itself. A/B tests usually fiddle with words. The bigger win is testing one framework against another — PAS vs AIDA on the same landing page routinely produces double-digit differences.

A framework doesn't write your copy — it decides what your copy does next. That's 80% of the job. The remaining 20% is specifics: real numbers, real names, real sentences a customer would say. Framework + specifics beats talent without either.
