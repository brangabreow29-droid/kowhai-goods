The 6 Formulas That Do the Heavy Lifting

You don't need advanced Excel to build a business plan spreadsheet — six formulas cover 95% of the work. Each is shown exactly as you'd type it:

1. Net profit per month

=B12-C12        (monthly revenue minus monthly expenses)

Trivial, but it's the foundation. Everything else builds on this row.

2. Cumulative cash (the number that finds your break-even)

=SUM($D$2:D2)-$F$1     (running total of net profit, minus total startup costs)

Drag it down all 12 rows. The first month this goes positive is the month your business has paid back its startup costs — the true break-even.

3. Break-even month detection

=COUNTIF(D2:D13,"<=0")+1     (counts non-positive months; the next one is break-even)

Returns "2" for the worked example below. One formula replaces an hour of squinting at a chart.

4. Break-even units per month (product businesses)

=C5/(C2-C3)     (fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit))

The worked candle example below returns 59 units/month. This single formula is the difference between guessing and knowing.

5. Loan repayment (if you're borrowing)

=ABS(PMT(7%/12, 60, 25000))     → $495.03/month

A $25,000 loan at 7% over 5 years costs $495/month. Put it in your expense forecast, not in your head.

6. Operating reserve

=AVERAGE(C2:C13)*3     (average monthly expenses × 3 months)

The cash cushion every lender asks about. The worked example below needs $2,572 set aside.

Break-Even Analysis for Product Businesses

The freelance example breaks even on cumulative profit. A product business breaks even on units per month, and the formula is the single most-quoted number in any business plan: break-even units = fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit).

Worked example — a candle business selling at $24 with $8.50 variable cost per unit (wax, jar, wick, fragrance, packaging) and $915/month fixed costs (workspace, insurance, website, ads):

InputValueExcel formula
Price per unit$24.00C2
Variable cost per unit$8.50C3
Contribution margin$15.50 (65%)=C2-C3
Monthly fixed costs$915C5
Break-even units/month59=C5/(C2-C3)
Break-even revenue/month$1,417=C5/(C2-C3)*C2

Two units a day covers the business. That's a number you can act on — sell at markets on weekends and you can see whether 59 units is a dream or a slow month. If the break-even looks unreachable, you have exactly three levers, and the spreadsheet lets you test each in seconds: raise the price (each $1 adds $1 to contribution margin), cut variable cost (bulk wax, simpler packaging), or cut fixed costs (drop the ads, share the workspace). Testing all three in a Word document means re-doing the math by hand every time.

How to Build Your Own Business Plan Excel Template in 30 Minutes

This is the free path. Open a blank workbook and build the core in this order:

  1. Startup Costs tab — list every one-off cost (equipment, legal, deposits) and every monthly cost in two blocks. Total each. Name the one-off total cell StartupCosts (Formulas → Define Name) so later formulas read cleanly.
  2. Revenue forecast — one row per product or service: price, then units sold per month across 12 columns. For services, model capacity honestly: one person delivering $3,500 websites realistically closes 1-3 per month, not 10.
  3. Expense forecast — rows per expense category, stepped up where volume drives cost. Total monthly expenses in a summary row.
  4. Net profit row — =revenue−expenses per month, then cumulative cash with =SUM($D$2:D2)-StartupCosts dragged across all 12 months.
  5. Break-even — for products: =fixed/(price−variable). For services: =COUNTIF(cum_cash_range,"<=0")+1 for the month number.
  6. Scenario block — three cells multiplying your Year 1 revenue by 0.55, 1.0, and 1.45, each subtracting the same expense base.
  7. Reserve — =AVERAGE(expense_row)*3. Fund it from early profits before anything else.

Then add the narrative tabs — Plan Info, Market Analysis, SWOT, Competitors, Strategy — as simple structured sheets with prompts. 6 Mistakes That Break Excel Business Plans

  • Hard-coded numbers inside formulas. The moment =3500*2 appears instead of =B3*C3, changing the price breaks nothing visibly and everything silently. Every input gets its own cell.
  • Hockey-stick revenue. Month 1 at $2,000 and month 12 at $40,000 with no mechanism (capacity, pipeline, ad spend) explaining the curve. Model how each sale happens, then count.
  • Expenses that never scale. If revenue triples and expenses stay flat, you've forgotten subcontractors, software seats, and payment fees — all of which scale with volume.
  • No tax line. A profitable-looking plan that ignores income tax overstates net by a quarter to a third. Add a tax row at your marginal rate; freelancers should also reserve for it separately.
  • Confusing profit with cash. A $9,000 invoice issued in month 4 and paid in month 6 is month-6 cash. If you invoice, add a payment-delay row — cumulative cash is what kills businesses, not cumulative profit.
  • Planning once. A business plan spreadsheet that isn't updated monthly becomes a historical document by week 6. The KPIs tab exists precisely so the plan stays the place you check.
